## s01
Right now, five NASA satellites are circling the Earth, watching for fire. In the last day alone, they reported tens of thousands of hot spots. Two of these satellites have watched for more than twenty years. But they do not see fire the same way.

## s02
We are Team Lumos, from Dhaka: M Al Shahriar Hameem, our team lead, with Tuasin Taj Fiha, Afifa Asif, Md Abu Ammar, Kausar Ahmed and Sabiha Akter. For the NASA Space Apps Challenge, we built Parallax Fire, to turn two satellite records that disagree into one honest story of when a place burns.

## s03
The older satellite, MODIS, sees the ground in pixels one kilometre wide. The sharper one, VIIRS, arrived in 2012 and sees pixels of 375 metres, so it catches smaller fires. In Bangladesh it records about five times as many.

## s04
So when you join the two records, burning seems to leap in 2012. Imagine planning your district’s fire season from this chart. You would ask for help against a change that never happened on the ground.

## s05
Parallax Fire puts both on one scale. It learns how the two satellites relate, only from years when both were flying. Press one switch, and most of that jump disappears. The camera changed, not the fire.

## s06
Here is a real test. In March 2024, the sharper satellite counted 621 detections over Bangladesh. The older one counted 111. That month was never used to build our estimate. From the older count alone, we estimated about 1,093, likely between 571 and 1,529. The real 621 fell inside that range.

## s07
First, pick a place. The calendar shows each year as a row and each month as a column. In Bangladesh, March and April hold about four in five detections, year after year. In California, the season moves to late summer.

## s08
Next, the map shows where it burned, district by district, on a real map.

## s09
We checked the method on the years 2023 and 2024, which it had never seen. In Bangladesh it missed by 98 detections a month, where the raw older count misses by 627. In New South Wales it was not good enough, so there the app shows the raw records only, and says so.

## s10
Finally, for today, the live Earth shows each fire satellite where it really is, with every hot spot it reported in the last twenty-four hours. It is all NASA data: FIRMS fire detections from MODIS and VIIRS, and GIBS satellite imagery, with public orbit records.

## s11
A planner can see the real burning season before deciding when to watch closely. A researcher can compare two decades without mistaking a new camera for new fire. Looking ahead, we want more regions, the newer NOAA satellites, a Bangla interface, and partners to test it with.

## s12
Parallax Fire. Same fires, two cameras, one honest record. This is Team Lumos. Thank you.
