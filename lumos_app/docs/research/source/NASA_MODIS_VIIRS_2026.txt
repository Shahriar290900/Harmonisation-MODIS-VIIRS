Executive summary
The challenge is a sensor-harmonization problem before it is a mapping problem. MODIS provides a long record at 
roughly 1 km nominal fire-pixel resolution; VIIRS provides a newer record at roughly 375 m and detects many smaller 
hotspots. Raw hotspot counts therefore create a false discontinuity when the observing system changes. A strong 
solution should explicitly correct, quantify, and visualize that discontinuity.
Recommended product  Build a global web application whose flagship output is a 2000-to-present Burning Activity 
Calendar for a selected area of interest (AOI). Pair the calendar with a Sensor Lab that shows raw MODIS vs VIIRS, 
the overlap-based calibration, uncertainty, and validation. Use Bangladesh as the first demonstration AOI and at 
least one contrasting international AOI.
 Must-use data: NASA FIRMS standard/archive MODIS Terra/Aqua and VIIRS S-NPP/NOAA-20/NOAA-21 active-fire 
records.
 Strong validation/context: MCD64A1 and VNP64A1 burned area; land-cover stratification; optional GlobFire/Global Fire 
Atlas event products.
 Best scientific differentiator: resolution-aware, season-aware and land-cover-aware overlap calibration instead of a 
single global count multiplier.
 Best UX differentiator: a calendar first, then map; every result exposes sensor mix, uncertainty, source/version, and 
why a month is considered unusual.
 What not to claim: do not call every hotspot a wildfire and do not present a historical anomaly score as an exact future-
fire prediction.
Hackathon timing from the provided Bangladesh submission portal screenshot: 240-second video and a publicly 
accessible source-code link, with the displayed submission end of 07 Oct 2026, 11:59 PM. Re-check the portal 
timezone and status before final submission.

1. Challenge interpretation
NASA describes the task as building a web application that harmonizes active-fire hotspot records from different 
sensors into a burning-activity calendar, so early-warning responders, scientists and land managers can examine 
historical patterns, unusual conditions and critical periods inside a selected AOI.
Official 2026 challenge page - Open link - Primary challenge reference
What "harmonize" means in practice
 Do not concatenate rows and stop. VIIRS sees much smaller/higher-resolution thermal anomalies than MODIS; raw 
counts are not a stable long-term measure.
 Create a common analytical unit. Aggregate both sensors to the same AOI/grid and time unit before calibration.
 Use the overlap period. From 2012 onward MODIS and S-NPP VIIRS overlap, allowing empirical calibration rather than 
guessed conversion factors.
 Preserve sensor provenance. The user should always be able to see whether a historical value was observed directly, 
calibrated from MODIS, or generated from a mixed-sensor period.
 Expose uncertainty. A harmonized historical estimate is not identical to a direct modern VIIRS observation. Show 
confidence/uncertainty rather than hiding this difference.
Judge-facing one-liner  "We turn two incompatible satellite eras into one transparent, uncertainty-aware fire-
activity calendar - without pretending a 1 km MODIS detection is the same thing as a 375 m VIIRS detection."

5. Scientific harmonization method
The method below is designed to be defensible within a hackathon: transparent baseline first, more flexible model only 
if validation proves it helps.
5.1 Why raw counts fail
VIIRS 375 m can detect substantially more small hotspots than MODIS 1 km, and the difference also depends on land 
cover, fire intensity, season, view geometry and time of overpass. Published comparisons show that relationships in 
FRP and detection rates vary by scale and ecosystem. A single universal "VIIRS = k x MODIS" coefficient is therefore 
easy to implement but scientifically fragile.
5.2 Recommended pipeline
1. Ingest and freeze provenance: Download standard FIRMS historical records. Add source product, 
collection/version, satellite, acquisition UTC and ingestion date to every row.
2. Normalize schema: Map all sensors into one canonical table. Keep raw fields unchanged and add derived columns 
separately.
3. AOI and spatial grid: For a fast MVP: aggregate at AOI-day/month. Better: use H3 or a 5 km equal-area-ish working 
grid so both sensors are compared on the same support.
4. Quality filtering: Flag low confidence and non-vegetation/static thermal sources where product/version permits. Do 
not silently delete; keep counts before/after filtering.
5. Deduplicate / cluster: Within each sensor/day, cluster nearby detections if the UI presents "fire systems" rather 
than raw pixels. DBSCAN/HDBSCAN is reasonable, but keep raw pixel counts available.
6. Build overlap pairs: 2012-present is the calibration era. Aggregate MODIS and VIIRS at matched AOI/grid-time 
units. Where feasible use near-simultaneous overpasses; otherwise use day/month units and mark this limitation.
7. Transparent baseline: Estimate robust seasonal scale factors using medians or a robust regression, preferably 
stratified by month and land cover. This creates a VIIRS-equivalent historical MODIS activity estimate.
8. Optional improved model: Fit a robust GLM/GAM/Huber/negative-binomial model using MODIS count, MODIS 
FRP, month, land cover and scan/track/view proxies. Try gradient boosting only if it improves held-out years without 
destroying interpretability.
9. Build activity metrics: Keep at least two harmonized outputs: detection activity and FRP activity. Convert to 
monthly percentile/z-score only for intuitive calendar display.
10. Quantify uncertainty: Bootstrap calibration years or report fold-to-fold error/CV. Mark pre-2012 values as model-
adjusted, not direct VIIRS observations.
11. Validate: Hold out recent years and compare fire-season timing/events against burned-area products/event data. 
Report MAE/RMSE/correlation and seasonal peak agreement.
12. Precompute: Write compact Parquet/JSON tables consumed by the web app. Live API calls should cover recent 
observations, not the whole historical archive.
5.3 Baseline formulas you can explain in 20 seconds
Seasonal calibration baseline  For month m and overlap years y, compute a robust ratio r_m = median[(VIIRS 
activity + epsilon) / (MODIS activity + epsilon)]. For pre-VIIRS years, estimate VIIRS-equivalent activity as MODIS x 
r_m. Use cross-validation and show the variability of r_m.
A more stable alternative for sparse cells is a regression on log1p counts/FRP rather than raw ratios. For the calendar, 
display a percentile relative to that AOI and calendar month: e.g., "92nd historical percentile for April". That avoids 
implying a universal physical unit when the product is really a normalized activity indicator.
5.4 What to call the output
 Harmonized hotspot activityGood default term; clearly tied to observation.
 VIIRS-equivalent historical activityGood for calibrated pre-2012 estimates if that is genuinely your target scale.

What the literature implies for your method
 Resolution bias is fundamental: VIIRS 375 m has a greater response to relatively small fires than MODIS 1 km.
 FRP helps but is not magically sensor-invariant: published comparisons show FRP differences vary by scale, season, 
ecosystem and viewing geometry.
 Use matched observations when possible: near-simultaneous comparisons are stronger than arbitrary same-month 
point matching.
 Land cover matters: low-biomass/cropland and forests can show different omission behavior; stratification is defensible.
 Aggregate before overfitting: a stable 5 km/day or AOI/day comparison is usually more realistic for a four-day 
hackathon than pixel-perfect cross-sensor physics.
 Show uncertainty: sensor transfer is an estimation problem, not a perfect conversion.

14. Risks, caveats and scientific honesty
Risk Why it matters Mitigation
Hotspot != wildfire FIRMS thermal anomalies may include 
agricultural burning, volcanoes, gas flares, hot 
smoke or other hot sources.
Use "hotspot" / "burning activity" by default; 
filter/flag type and persistent sources; only call 
an event wildfire when supported.
No detection != no fire Cloud cover, overpass timing, sensor 
saturation/geometry and thresholds can hide 
activity.
Represent missing/low-coverage separately 
from zero; show data-quality flags.
Sensor resolution mismatch VIIRS detects smaller hotspots than MODIS. Calibrate in overlap period on common 
space/time units; never compare raw counts as 
a long trend.
